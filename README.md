# DSP to JSFX

Open-source DSP ported to JSFX for REAPER. Every plugin's header names the original it was ported from and its license.

## Install

There are two ways to install the plugins. Use one or the other, not both.

### ReaPack (recommended, keeps everything up to date)

1. In REAPER, open **Extensions → ReaPack → Import repositories…** and paste:

   ```
   https://github.com/chmaha/DSP-Ports-JSFX/raw/main/index.xml
   ```

2. Open **Extensions → ReaPack → Browse packages…**, filter by **DSP to JSFX**, and install the plugins you want.
3. The plugins then appear in the FX browser under **JS**.

ReaPack also installs each plugin's `Dependencies` files and keeps everything up to date.

### ReaperPkg bundles (no ReaPack needed)

The plugins come in eight bundles, each with its own [release](https://github.com/chmaha/DSP-Ports-JSFX/releases) holding one `.ReaperPkg` file. Each bundle installs a whole set of plugins with their `Dependencies` files:

- **chmaha Instruments**: the synths, pianos, organs and other sampled and modelled instruments.
- **chmaha Effects, MIDI and Utility**: effects, MIDI tools and utilities.
- **chmaha Convolution Reverbs**: Brick by Brick, Word for Word and Wall to Wall, with their impulse responses.
- **chmaha Metering and Visualization**: meters, analysers and visualizers.
- **chmaha Spatial Audio**: binaural, ambisonic and headphone plugins.
- **Evangelina Symphonica**: the orchestral series.
- **Rosalina Curiosa**: the VCSL series.
- **Break Room**: the mini-games plugin and its music.

Download the `.ReaperPkg` file and open it in REAPER to install. The plugins then appear in the FX browser under **JS: DSP to JSFX/Plugin Ports**. Bundles don't update themselves: download the newer bundle when a new release comes out.

### Manual install

Copy the plugin's `.jsfx` file into your REAPER `Effects` folder (**Options → Show REAPER resource path…**). For every plugin that has files in `Dependencies` (the drum kits, the unplugred ports and others), also copy the `Dependencies` folder that sits next to the `.jsfx` (each plugin needs its own subfolder, such as `Drumkits`, `Scope` or `VU`), and keep it next to the `.jsfx`.

## User guide

See the [DSP to JSFX manual (PDF)](https://chmaha.github.io/DSP-Ports-JSFX/DSP-to-JSFX-Manual.pdf) for a quick start to each plugin's controls, with screenshots of every plugin.

## Instruments

- **Synthy McSynthface**: four instruments in one (ePiano, JX10, DX10 and Piano), ported from the mda plugins via [mda-lv2](https://gitlab.com/drobilla/mda-lv2).
- **Jexed**: DX7-style FM synthesizer, ported from [Dexed](https://github.com/asb2m10/dexed). Includes 1056 voices (Dexed_01 and SynprezFM_01–32 cartridges) and a full voice editor with operator copy/paste and a simple ADSR mode for setting several envelopes at once.
- **JamSynth**: subtractive synthesizer with 28 factory banks and a 128-slot user bank, ported from [amsynth](https://github.com/amsynth/amsynth).
- **Herd Mentality**: subtractive synthesizer, ported from Calf Monosynth by Krzysztof Foltman ([Calf Studio Gear](https://calf-studio-gear.org)) and styled after [calfNXT](https://calfnxt.org) by Markus Schmidt. Two wavetable oscillators with 16 waveforms each (Osc1 with Stretch and Window, Osc2 with eight-voice unison), eight filter types, two envelopes, two LFOs and a ten-slot modulation matrix. Mono mode is Calf Monosynth exactly, with portamento and four legato modes; Poly mode (an addition) plays up to eight voices. Includes Calf's 21 factory presets, velocity to filter and amplitude, mod wheel, channel pressure, pitch bend and a sustain pedal.
- **Six Appeal**: six-voice polysynth in the style of the Roland Juno-106, ported from [Ultramaster KR-106](https://github.com/kayrockscreenprinting/ultramaster_kr106) by Karl LaRocca and David Mansfield. Two engines, each with its own 128-patch factory bank: the 106 engine models the firmware (integer envelopes, LFO and filter CV at the 234 Hz main-loop rate) and the 60 engine models the analogue circuits. Includes the BBD chorus, an arpeggiator that can follow REAPER's tempo, Poly 1, Poly 2 and Mono key assignment, portamento and hold, 6, 8 or 10 voices, per-voice component variance with analogue noise, mains ripple and BBD clock noise, and MIDI CC and SysEx (IPR/APR) in and out.
- **Tolerable Tines**: electric piano, ported from Ardour's [ACE Reasonable Synth](https://github.com/Ardour/ardour/tree/master/libs/plugins/reasonablesynth.lv2).
- **Fork Lift**: physically modelled tine electric piano in the style of a 1970s 73-key stage piano, ported from [Frodhes](https://github.com/leonardoMirabal/frodhes) by Leonardo Mirabal. Each key is a tine and tone bar ringing as resonant modes, struck by a simulated hammer and read by a magnetic pickup, with four voicings (Stage, Suitcase, Early Felt and Bell Dyno), the Suitcase's stereo pan vibrato or a tremolo, release velocity and a sustain pedal with half-pedalling and re-pedalling. Every key has its own tine, so there is no voice limit. Plays E1 to E7, or the full 88 keys.
- **Wurly Bird**: physically modelled Wurlitzer 200A electric piano, ported from [OpenWurli](https://github.com/hal0zer0/openwurli) by hal0zer0. Each of the 64 reeds is a modal oscillator struck by a felt hammer and read by the electrostatic pickup, followed by circuit models of the preamp (with the tremolo inside its feedback loop), the volume network, the power amp and, optionally, the speaker cabinet. Velocity sets the hammer force, the sustain pedal has half-pedalling, and optional MLP corrections adjust each note's partials. Plays A1 to C7, or the full 88 keys.
- **Partiality**: additive and modulation synthesizer, ported from the ADsynth engine of [ZynAddSubFX](https://github.com/zynaddsubfx/zynaddsubfx). Includes eight voices per note, each with its own harmonic oscillator and modulator, unison, noise sources, envelopes and LFOs, five filter types, poly, mono and legato modes, 19 factory patches and 122 instruments converted from the ZynAddSubFX banks.
- **Pad Thai**: pad synthesizer, ported from the PADsynth engine of [ZynAddSubFX](https://github.com/zynaddsubfx/zynaddsubfx). Every harmonic of a drawn oscillator spectrum is spread into a band of frequencies with a shaped profile, and long random-phase wavetables are built from the result, giving smooth choirs, strings and evolving pads. Includes the harmonic profile, bandwidth and bandwidth scale, overtone positions for bells and metal, a paintable resonance curve, bandwidth, discrete and continuous modes, table size and spacing, amplitude, filter and pitch envelopes and LFOs, analog, formant, state-variable, Moog and comb filters, punch, glide, poly, mono and legato modes, the ZynAddSubFX MIDI controller set and 107 instruments converted from the ZynAddSubFX banks. Wavetables are rebuilt in the background while you edit, and a cubic interpolation option is added for smoother high notes.
- **Console Yourself**: chip-synth instrument with the sound hardware of six game consoles: NES (with the Famicom VRC6 and VRC7 expansion chips, from [Nes_Snd_Emu](http://slack.net/~ant/libs/audio.html) and [ymfm](https://github.com/aaronsgiles/ymfm)), Game Boy, Master System / Game Gear and PC Engine (from [MAME](https://github.com/mamedev/mame)), Mega Drive (YM2612 or YM3438 FM with a patch editor, from ymfm) and Super NES (S-DSP with echo, from [snes_spc](http://slack.net/~ant/libs/audio.html), playing Doug Fraker's 203 [BRR samples](https://github.com/nesdoug/SNES_13)). Layer mode stacks up to 16 chips for chords (or plays a single channel, such as the NES triangle for bass), with drums on MIDI channel 10; Tracker mode maps MIDI channels to the chip's hardware channels. The Game Boy and PC Engine waves can be drawn by hand.
- **Off the Cuff**: Yamaha OPL FM synthesizer with three chips, the YM3526 (OPL), YM3812 (OPL2, as on the AdLib and Sound Blaster) and YMF262 (OPL3, as on the Sound Blaster Pro 2 and 16), ported from [KoniSynth](https://github.com/k0n1m4k1/KoniSynth)'s OPL engine by k0n1m4k1, which follows the chip behaviour documented by Nuked-OPL3. Includes two-operator voices with an optional second voice per note, OPL3 four-operator voices, rhythm mode drums on MIDI channel 10, multitimbral General MIDI through the DMX driver of Doom, Heretic and Hexen with the [DMXOPL3](https://github.com/sneakernets/DMXOPL) bank by sneakernets (175 instruments and drums, also loadable as single sounds), six KoniSynth presets and the AdLib Gold tone, stereo and surround stage (TDA8425_emu and YM7128B_emu by Andrea Zoppi).
- **Byte Me**: Commodore 64 SID chip synthesizer for chiptunes, ported from [reSID](https://github.com/libsidplayfp/resid) by Dag Lem. Three voices with the 6581 or 8580 sound, hard sync, ring modulation and the analog filter, in Lead (all three voices on one note), Poly (three-note chords, with two or three layered voices per note) or Multi mode (MIDI channels 1-3 on voices 1-3, channel 4 for digi drums). Each voice has a 16-step table of waveform, note, pulse-width and cutoff steps that runs at the C64's 50 or 60 Hz frame rate or the project tempo, plus an arpeggiator, a SID or digi drum kit on the GM drum notes, up to four chips for wide stereo or 12-voice poly, chorus and stereo delay, 199 factory patches and a 128-slot user bank.
- **Calculated Risk**: the Casio VL-1 (VL-Tone, 1979), a monophonic pocket keyboard with a rhythm box and a calculator, ported from [KoniSynth](https://github.com/k0n1m4k1/KoniSynth)'s VL-1 engine by k0n1m4k1, which takes its waveforms, envelope, output stage and rhythm sounds from the [PolyValens VL-1 emulator](https://github.com/linuxmao-org/VL1-emulator), with the emulator's panel logic. Includes the five preset sounds and the ADSR sound code entered on the calculator, ten rhythms, melody recording with One Key Play and Auto Play, the demo song, a working calculator and the segment display.
- **Daisy Bell**: an experimental, slightly wacky text-to-speech instrument with three vintage speech engines: SAM (the 1982 Software Automatic Mouth, from Sebastian Macke's reverse-engineered C version), a Klatt formant synthesizer from rsynth, and HAL, a port of [Flite](http://www.festvox.org/flite/) (CMU Festival Lite). It speaks whatever arrives as MIDI text events: type words into REAPER's MIDI editor (text or lyric events) and Daisy Bell says them as the playhead passes, or holds each one until the next note and speaks or sings it at that note's pitch. Robotic, crunchy and often hard to understand, as these voices always were, but good for vocoder fodder, retro robot lines, sound design and the odd sung melody. Click the face on the screen for a demo.
- **Dr. Nine**: drum synthesizer, ported from [9W9](https://github.com/athousanddetails/schwung-9W9).
- **Skin in the Game**: drum voice, ported from the 4ms [Drum and Djembe](https://github.com/4ms/metamodule-core-modules) modules (4ms CoreModules for MetaModule and VCV Rack). Drum is a two-operator FM drum with pitch, FM, tone and noise envelopes; Djembe is a 20-mode resonant hand drum struck by filtered noise (from Faust's pm.djembe). Any MIDI note strikes the drum and holding it holds the gate. Key Track lets the note set the pitch with up to four voices, and an optional Velocity switch scales each hit.
- **Ensemble Cast**: additive drone oscillator, ported from the 4ms [Ensemble Oscillator](https://github.com/4ms/metamodule-core-modules) (4ms CoreModules for MetaModule and VCV Rack, original firmware by Matthias Puech). Up to 16 pairs of sine oscillators per voice, tuned to one of ten scales or one you teach it, with spread, balance, detune, cross FM, twist and warp shaping, freeze and stereo splits. It always sounds; MIDI notes transpose up to four voices.
- **Strum und Drang**: chord harp and rhythm box in the style of the Suzuki Omnichord OM-84, ported from [KoniSynth](https://github.com/k0n1m4k1/KoniSynth)'s Omnichord engine. 84 chords from three rows of chord buttons (major, minor and 7th, combined for dim, maj7, m7 and aug), played from the keyboard as the hardware's button grid or by intervals, a 13-string strum plate on the keys above the split point or the mod wheel, a held chord voice, auto bass, 12 rhythms on CR-78 drum voices that sync to REAPER, and a plate reverb.
- **Kick Back**: analog rhythm machine with three kits: the Roland TR-808, ported from [Mz808](https://github.com/simozzer/Mz808) by Simon Moscrop (bridged-T resonators after Werner, Abel and Smith) and calibrated against Michael Fischer's recordings of a real TR-808, and the Roland CR-78 and Korg Mini Pops 7, ported from [KoniSynth](https://github.com/k0n1m4k1/KoniSynth)'s drum machine engine. The TR-808 panel has every level, tone, tuning, decay and snappy knob of the hardware. Includes 29 rhythm patterns that sync to REAPER, a second pattern to combine with the first, swing, a preview that plays while REAPER is stopped, eight user rhythms with an editor, MIDI out, clickable pads with step lights, named notes in the piano roll and an optional 11-output multi-out.
- **Acid Reign**: TB-303-style acid bass synth, ported from [Open303](https://github.com/RobinSchmidt/Open303). Includes the accent and slide behaviour, the Devil Fish envelope and slide mods, 16 filter modes and a 16-pattern step sequencer that follows a held key (Key Sync) or REAPER's transport (Host Sync).
- **Ring Leader**: resonator, ported from Mutable Instruments [Rings](https://github.com/pichenettes/eurorack/tree/master/rings). Modal resonator, sympathetic strings, inharmonic string, FM voice, quantized sympathetic strings and string + reverb, with 1, 2 or 4 voices, played from MIDI or excited by the track's audio, plus the module's hidden Disastrous Peace string synth.
- **Gong with the Wind**: gong, ported from the Gong model and Gong beater of Steve Harris's [swh-plugins](https://github.com/swh/lv2). Eight nonlinear waveguides, four spokes and four rim arcs, each with its own size and two stiffness controls, plus inner and outer damping and a movable mic. Strike it with any MIDI note, by clicking the gong, or with transients in the track's audio. Fixes two upstream bugs: the damping filter now cuts the highs instead of the lows, and the beater is reset for each strike.
- **Plait Tectonics**: macro oscillator, ported from Mutable Instruments [Plaits](https://github.com/pichenettes/eurorack/tree/master/plaits). Includes all 24 models of the current firmware: virtual analog (with and without filter), phase distortion, three banks of six-operator FM patches, wave terrain, string machine, chiptune, waveshaping, two-operator FM, granular formants, additive, wavetables, chords, speech, swarm, filtered and particle noise, inharmonic string, modal resonator and analog-style kick, snare and hi-hat. Played from MIDI through the module's low-pass gate.
- **In Your Element**: modal synthesizer, ported from Mutable Instruments [Elements](https://github.com/pichenettes/eurorack/tree/master/elements). Bow, blow and strike exciters feed a 64-mode resonator (or a string, or five strings tuned to a chord), followed by stereo spread and reverb, plus the module's hidden Ominous Voice. Playable from MIDI with velocity and pressure.
- **Splitting Hairs**: macro oscillator, ported from Mutable Instruments [Braids](https://github.com/pichenettes/eurorack/tree/master/braids). All 47 models, from CSAW, folded, synced and supersaw oscillators through VOSIM, vowels, FM and physical models to drums, wavetables and noise, with the module's fixed-point firmware running bit for bit at 96 kHz, its settings menu (bit and rate reduction, AD envelope routings, quantiser, drift) and the 14-segment display.
- **Home Truths**: 8-bit hybrid monosynth, ported from Mutable Instruments [Shruthi-1](https://github.com/pichenettes/shruthi-1) by Emilie Gillet (firmware 1.03 with the digital DSP filter board). Two oscillators with 35 shapes, from classic waves to CZ, FM, wavetables, vowels and noise, combined by 14 operators, plus a sub oscillator and transients, two envelopes, two LFOs, a 12-slot modulation matrix with two CV operators, a 16-step sequencer and arpeggiator that can follow REAPER's transport, the DSP board's 3-pole low-pass or high-pass filter and 16 effects, and the 128 factory patches and sequences. Patch up to eight modules (dynamics, filters, EQ, delays, modulation, pitch, distortion, reverb, generators and utilities) with cables from IN to OUT for serial chains, parallel blends, sidechains from channels 3/4 and modulation patches, with latency compensation. Includes GVerb (Juhana Sadeharju) and the GSM 06.10 codec (Jutta Degener and Carsten Bormann). Fixes over twenty upstream bugs, among them the Reverse Delay that was always one sample long, flanger and chorus blow-ups, out-of-bounds reads at high sample rates and the Pitch Scaler dropping half the spectrum. Requires REAPER 7 or later.
- **Mixed Messages**: sound mangler, ported from Hermes Filter in [swh-plugins](https://github.com/swh/lv2) by Steve Harris. Two oscillators, two LFOs and three ring modulators are mixed with the input and split into three bands, each with its own drive, a state-variable filter (LP, HP, BP, BR or AP) swept by both LFOs, a feedback delay of up to 2 s and a gain. Fixes the upstream bugs that left the filters and both LFOs doing nothing, gave every band Band 1's drive and made the sound depend on the block size, and adds real 3x oversampling.
- **Lumina Channel Strip**: tape, HPF/LPF, gate, analog EQ, compressor, saturation, limiter and true peak metering in one plugin, built from the ports above.
- **Channel Surfing**: analog-style channel strip, ported from [AnalogChannel](https://github.com/FilTer87/VST-AnalogChannel) by Filippo Terenzi (KuramaSound). Saturation in (Clean, Pure, Tape, Tube), high- and low-pass filters, a clean compressor, a low-level expander or lifter, a Baxandall-style four-band EQ, a Warm or Punch style compressor, five console colours, an output stage with clippers and the output level, every section switchable without clicks, plus per-channel console variation from 24 channel pairs. Built on Airwindows, JClones CL1B and LOSER algorithms.
- **Original design**: Dynamic Tilt, a tilt EQ whose slope follows the signal.
- **Board Meeting** (original design): analog console colour for every channel, group bus and the master, with each copy behaving like its own channel strip. An input transformer that saturates the lows harder, a gently asymmetric amp stage (2x oversampled, zero latency) and a console EQ curve, in Channel, Bus or Master mode (Bus and Master add a soft-knee, stereo-linked glue compressor) and Warm, Punchy or Silky flavour. Drive is gain-compensated, so it changes the character, not the level. Every copy gets its own channel number, which gives it slightly different tolerances like a real desk. Optional subtle tape and a realistic console noise floor (after CNF Console Noise Floor by Heavy Water Audio) with six levels, from Pristine to Portastudio hiss. VU meters, gain-reduction lamps and a smooth bypass.
- **Broadband Denoise & Restoration** (original design): six-band downward expander for steady broadband noise such as hiss, tape noise and room noise. The bands sit at log-spaced frequencies from Low Freq to High Freq, each with its own threshold and range. Thresholds are set by hand or follow each band's noise floor (adaptive, with slow adaptation and reset), plus HF bias, stereo link, optional high and low pass at the band limits, mix and output. Per-band delta solo and a global delta play only the noise that is removed, and a display shows the gain reduction across the spectrum.

## calfNXT

The complete [calfNXT](https://calfnxt.org) suite by Markus Schmidt (derived from Calf Studio Gear by Krzysztof Foltman and Thor Harald Johansen), apart from the Impulse convolver. Every plugin keeps the calfNXT look, with dark and light themes and five accent colours.

- **Aligner NXT**: eight-lane time aligner and mixer for multi-microphone recordings. Per-lane delay trim, join, polarity, gain, pan, mute and solo, a stereo reference input on channels 9/10, hit waveforms and per-lane correlation through an analysis band-pass, and a master delay reported as delay compensation.
- **Analyzer NXT**: stereo spectrum (1k to 8k FFT, three scales) with a difference view, BS.1770-4 loudness (momentary, short-term, integrated, range and true peak) with EBU, ATSC, -14 and -16 LUFS targets, level meters, correlation and a goniometer.
- **Bender NXT**: pitch-pedal shifter over +/-24 semitones with optional semitone or whole-tone snapping, glide, grain quality, tone and mix.
- **Chorus NXT**: stereo chorus with one to eight voices, phase spread and overlap, stereo phase, wet high and low pass and a channel selector.
- **Compressor NXT**: feed-forward compressor with peak, RMS and opto detection, soft knee, programme-dependent release, mix, stereo link modes and an external sidechain with 12 to 48 dB/oct filters and listen.
- **Crusher NXT**: bit crusher with continuous bit depth from 1 to 16 (linear or logarithmic), anti-aliasing, DC asymmetry and mix.
- **DeEsser NXT**: de-esser with a filtered detector, wide or split-band reduction, a Rumble mode that turns it into a de-rumbler, channel routing and detector listen.
- **Delay NXT**: tempo-based stereo delay with tap tempo or host sync, Stereo, Ping-Pong, L then R and R then L routings, filtered feedback, stereo width and an echo-pattern chart.
- **Denoiser NXT**: spectral noise reducer with three learnable noise profiles that can be blended, amount, floor, span and offset, a band limit, profile listen and a listen to what is removed.
- **Elliptic NXT**: bass mono maker. A Linkwitz-Riley crossover (12 or 24 dB/oct, 30 to 200 Hz) splits the side signal and Amount pulls the side bass to the centre, while the mid runs through the same filters so the mono sum stays flat. Listen solos the side bass being removed; a goniometer shows the bass band and a correlation meter the full output.
- **Equalizer NXT**: 16-band equalizer (parametric, shelves, pass filters up to 48 dB/oct) where every band works on stereo, left, right, mid or side and can turn dynamic with its own sidechain compressor.
- **Expander NXT**: downward expander and gate with range, hysteresis, hold, a filtered detector, external sidechain and two inhibit keys that keep the gate shut on spill.
- **Filter NXT**: multimode filter (low, high, band pass, band reject and allpass) with resonance, soft clip, mix, inertia, an envelope follower and MIDI note tracking.
- **Flanger NXT**: stereo flanger with feedback, stereo phase and a comb-filter chart.
- **Harmonics NXT**: exciter and saturator: a filtered feed band goes into a transistor-to-tube waveshaper with up to 4x oversampling, and only the added harmonics are mixed back in. Wide, Exciter and Bass presets.
- **Limiter NXT**: look-ahead brickwall limiter with up to 4x oversampling, true peak margin, adaptive release, three gain curves, release hold, transient emphasis, colour, auto level and a difference listen.
- **Multiband Compressor NXT**: two to six bands with flat-summing Linkwitz-Riley crossovers, a full compressor, bypass and listen per band, channel routing and mono.
- **Multiband Limiter NXT**: two to six bands of look-ahead limiting with per-band release and weight, a broadband limiter on the sum, true peak, oversampling, auto level and band and difference listen.
- **Octaver NXT**: pitch-tracking octaver with one and two octaves down, one octave up and a sub oscillator, each with level, balance, tone, formant and listen.
- **Phaser NXT**: stereo phaser with up to 12 stages, feedback, stereo phase and a response chart.
- **Pulsator NXT**: tremolo and auto-pan with five wave shapes, pulse width, per-channel phase, tap tempo or host sync.
- **Reverb NXT**: algorithmic reverb with image-source early reflections of a room you size, a modulated late tail with RT60 decay and damping, input filters, air, wet widening, ducking, gate and freeze.
- **Ringmod NXT**: ring modulator from 1 Hz to 20 kHz with four carrier waves, stereo phase and detune, and two LFOs that sweep the carrier, detune, LFO rate and amount.
- **Split NXT**: mono to stereo splitter with volume, mute and polarity on each side.
- **Stereo NXT**: stereo utility with a seven-mode channel matrix, mid and side level, pan and balance, a high-band side decorrelator, channel delay, stereo base, phase rotation, a goniometer and correlation.
- **Tamer NXT**: spectral resonance tamer that finds and cuts narrow peaks as they happen, with a search range, harmonic protection, an external sidechain and a listen to what is removed.
- **Transients NXT**: transient shaper with attack and sustain amounts, lookahead, soft clip, mix, delta listen and a filtered detector.
- **Tuner NXT**: real-time pitch correction to a scale or the notes held on a MIDI keyboard, with retune and release speed, vibrato and glide preservation, formant control, an optional synthetic vibrato and a pitch history chart.

## Spatial audio

- **All Ears**: binaural panner for headphones, ported from [SPARTA](https://github.com/leomccormack/SPARTA) Binauraliser by Leo McCormack. Places up to 64 input channels around the listener with the HRTFs of a KEMAR dummy head (Genelec Aural ID, 836 directions), interpolated from the three nearest measurements. Drag sources in the panning window, load loudspeaker layouts from stereo to 22.2 (including 5.1 to 7.1.4 with an LFE channel fed to both ears), diffuse-field EQ and scene rotation by yaw, pitch and roll for head tracking.
- **Sphere of Influence**: ambisonic encoder, ported from SPARTA AmbiENC by Leo McCormack. Pans up to 64 input channels into a 1st to 7th order ambisonic scene, with click-free moves, ACN or FuMa channel order, N3D, SN3D or FuMa normalisation and the same loudspeaker presets.
- **Spin Doctor**: ambisonic rotator, ported from SPARTA Rotator by Leo McCormack. Turns a 1st to 7th order scene with exact spherical-harmonic rotation, by yaw, pitch and roll in either order or by a quaternion from a head tracker, with click-free cross-fades.
- **Ear Worm**: ambisonic decoder for headphones, ported from SPARTA AmbiBIN by Leo McCormack. Decodes a 1st to 7th order scene with the KEMAR HRTFs using LS, LS with diffuse-field EQ, spatial resampling, time alignment or MagLS, with max-rE weighting, diffuse covariance matching, truncation EQ, HRIR pre-processing and built-in rotation. New decoders are designed in the background while the old one keeps playing.
- **Speak Volumes**: ambisonic loudspeaker decoder, ported from SPARTA AmbiDEC by Leo McCormack. Decodes a 1st to 7th order scene to up to 64 loudspeakers with the SAD, MMD, EPAD or AllRAD designs, separate low- and high-frequency decoders split at an adjustable crossover, each with max-rE weighting and amplitude or energy normalisation, and a decoding order per frequency band with presets for the Zylia, Eigenmike and DTU microphone arrays. Loads SPARTA's loudspeaker layouts and REAPER surround layouts from 5.1 to 7.1.4 with a silent LFE channel, or place the speakers by azimuth and elevation, and listen to the layout on headphones with the KEMAR HRTFs.
- **Cross Talk**: headphone crossfeed, ported from [libbs2b](http://bs2b.sourceforge.net) by Boris Mikhaylov. Each ear gets a low-passed, attenuated copy of the other channel, as from a pair of speakers, with the bs2b Default, Chu Moy and Jan Meier presets, adjustable cutoff and feed, and an optional interaural delay after [Roof|control](https://github.com/Ilya-audio/roof_control) by Ilya Kuznetsov.

## MIDI

- **Spaghetti Junction**: modular rack for the 34 x42 MIDI filters, ported from [midifilter.lv2](https://github.com/x42/midifilter.lv2). Patch up to eight filters with cables from MIDI IN to MIDI OUT for serial chains, parallel branches, key splits and layers. Requires REAPER 7 or later.
- **Grid Expectations**: topographic drum sequencer, ported from Mutable Instruments [Grids](https://github.com/pichenettes/eurorack/tree/master/grids). Moves around a 5 x 5 map of drum patterns with fill and chaos for each part, or plays three euclidean rhythms. Follows REAPER's transport or runs free, and drives any drum instrument after it on the track.
- **Jezebel**: topographic drum sequencer, ported from Valley [Topograph](https://github.com/ValleyAudio/ValleyRackFree) by Dale Johnson (with its smaller sibling uGraph), itself based on Mutable Instruments Grids by Emilie Gillet. Map X and Map Y move around a 5 x 5 map of drum patterns, three Fill knobs set how busy each part is and Chaos adds random variation. Three sequencer modes: Henri (Topograph's own blend of the map), Original (the Grids blend) and Euclidean. Follows REAPER's transport at 4, 8 or 24 PPQN or runs free on its own tempo with swing, and plays three drum notes plus three optional extra notes (accents, or accent, clock and reset) with a separate accent velocity. Makes MIDI only and names its notes in the piano roll. The sidebar shows Alphonse Mucha's *The Pole Star* (1902), which comes gently to life while the transport plays.
- **Losing My Marbles**: random gate and melody generator, ported from Mutable Instruments [Marbles](https://github.com/pichenettes/eurorack/tree/master/marbles). Three gate streams and three random pitch streams with Deja Vu looping, seven gate models and six scales, plus a slower random value sent as a MIDI CC. Follows REAPER's transport or runs free.
- **Whale of a Time**: livecoding sequencer, ported from [Orca](https://github.com/hundredrabbits/Orca) by Hundred Rabbits (Devine Lu Linvega and Rekka Bellum). Every letter on a 64 x 32 grid is an operator that reads its neighbours and writes below it once per frame, so clocks, delays, tracks, euclidean rhythms and movers built from a few characters bang out MIDI notes, CCs and pitch bend for the instruments after it on the track. Follows REAPER's transport or runs free, with rewind on play, undo and redo, .orca file import, an operator help page and 37 example patches.

## Visualization

- **Sight for Sore Ears** (original design): audio-reactive visualizer with 25 scenes, from Plasma, Nebula and Starfield to Live Score (smoke rising from an 88-key piano), Cymatics, Galaxy and Harmonograph. It reacts to drums on the hit and to note onsets in music without drums, so classical and ambient pieces stay alive too. Includes 12 colour palettes and adjustable sensitivity, speed and quality. ## Utility

- **Continuum** (original design): endless roomtone from a few seconds of a quiet room. Two independent white-noise sources play through the recording as a convolution impulse response, so the room's own colour and air carry on for as long as you like, with no loops or repeats. Three rooms are built in (Rosemary Hall, Ivory Chapel and Dutch Church), or drop your own recording onto the window and choose which five seconds to use. The level matches the loudness of the recording, with an output trim and a mute. For filling gaps in dialogue, classical and podcast edits.
- **Beep Show** (original design, after the feature set of Voxengo Beeper): preview watermarker that puts beeps, low-passed noise bursts or dips in level into audio at a set interval, to protect demos, previews and stems. The material dips during each insertion so the output stays below its peak, and Variation randomises timing, duration, frequency and gain so the marks are hard to remove. The pattern follows the project position, so every render is identical. Zero latency, any number of channels.
- **Side Hustle** (original design, after the feature set of Voxengo MSED): mid-side encoder, decoder and inline mid-side processor. Gain, mute and pan for the mid and side channels, channel swap, polarity flips for both channels or the side alone, correlation and balance meters and a plasma vector scope that draws each octave band at its own radius. Zero latency.
- **Stereo Typical** (original design, after the controls of FLUX:: Stereo Tool): stereo imaging tool with input gain, polarity, mute and pan for each channel, stereo width from mono to extra wide, rotation of the whole image, ten instant presets, input and output peak meters, a phase correlation meter and a goniometer.
- **Image Conscious**: stereo image adjuster and mid-side matrix, ported from mda Image via [mda-effects](https://github.com/pluginguru/mda-effects). Decode SM or MS to LR, adjust LR or encode LR to MS, with side width (negative flips the side), side pan, mid level and mid pan, and a display of the resulting mixing matrix. LR to MS uses the standard side polarity (the original swapped left and right after a round trip).

## Break Room

**Break Room** (original design) is a collection of 15 little games to play when you need a breather, grouped into Zen and Games on the home screen. They range from calm pieces such as Koi Pond, Rain Window and Pixel Garden to Peggy, Krypton Gigablast, Flop House (Texas Hold'em against twelve rogues) and Hold Your Horses (the whole British racing year). Progress is saved with the project. Audio passes through untouched, and game sound only plays while the transport is stopped or paused, so it never ends up in a render or recording.

See the [Break Room manual (PDF)](https://chmaha.github.io/DSP-Ports-JSFX/BreakRoom-Manual.pdf) for a guide to every game.
