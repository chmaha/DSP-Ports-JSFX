Wall to Wall - impulse responses

Wall to Wall bundles five free impulse response libraries as packaged in the x42 zeroconvo IR
bundles by Robin Gareus (openairlib-ir.lv2, imreverbs-ir.lv2, airwindows-ir.lv2, ccgb-ir.lv2,
jezwells-ir.lv2), with the x42 preset gains and predelays. Files were renamed; OpenAIR files above 48 kHz were
resampled to 48 kHz (see section 1), and real recordings (OpenAIR, Concertgebouw, Jez Wells) that end
in a long flat measurement noise floor had that noise (only) removed, with a short fade inside the noise. All other
audio is unchanged from the x42 copies. Per-file details: WallToWall-CREDITS.txt.

Wall to Wall is free software and is given away at no charge. The impulse responses are not sold.


----------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. OpenAIR (files openair-*.flac)

Impulse responses from OpenAIR, the Open Acoustic Impulse Response Library, AudioLab, University of
York, www.openairlib.net (https://www.openair.hosted.york.ac.uk), contributed by the recordists named
for each space in WallToWall-CREDITS.txt.

Licensed under the Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International licence (CC BY 4.0),
https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/ , as stated in the x42 openairlib-ir.lv2 README:

  "These are impulse responses from www.openairlib.net
   A library of acoustic (IRs) from interesting buildings, spaces and other sources.
   Licensed in terms of the https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/"

Changes: converted to FLAC and level-matched by x42 (preset gain). In Wall to Wall the files are
renamed, and every file recorded above 48 kHz (96 and 192 kHz) has been resampled to 48 kHz (FLAC,
16-bit for 16-bit sources, 24-bit otherwise) with the plugin's own resampler; recordings that end in a
long flat measurement noise floor have that noise (only) removed, with a short fade inside the noise. WallToWall-CREDITS.txt marks each
one ("resampled to 48 kHz", "noise tail trimmed").
The gain applied at playback is the x42 preset gain (scaled for the rate change), except for the three
Virtual Membranes programs, which are trimmed to the library median. Mono-IR duplicates of stereo
recordings are not included. No endorsement by OpenAIR, the University of York or the recordists is
implied.

3. Airwindows (files airwindows-*.flac)

Convolution reverb impulses by Chris Johnson, Airwindows (https://www.airwindows.com), from
"AboutTheImpulses.pdf": "These impulses are please-buy-my-products-ware. They themselves are totally
free and unencumbered". Thank you, Chris.

4. Concertgebouw Brugge (files concertgebouw-*.flac)

From the x42 ccgb-ir.lv2 README:

  1200 seat modern concert hall.
  All IR's are strictly non-commercial and not for sale.
  
  This is a re-pack of files from
  https://www.dropbox.com/s/jq8oj2pudphhtwl/concertgebouw.zip?dl=1
  
  Stage FL (front left) and FR (front right) were combined into a
  four-channel full-stereo IR file. Likewise BL (back left) and
  BR (back right) were combined. In either case on the front facing
  stereo pair was used.
  
  Back was only 3m50 back, as the stage was set on the 'chamber music' setting.
  These IR's were made at the 13th row (approx 9m from the stage, 3m high).
